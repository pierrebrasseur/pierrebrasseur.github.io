Claiming jurisdiction in the public arena : an ethnography of occupational legitimation in an unrecognised care occupation

Pierre Brasseur · Journal of Professions and Organization, à paraître

Abstract

How does an occupation construct a jurisdictional claim and professional legitimacy when no credential system, regulatory framework or professional body recognises it? This article addresses that question through an ethnography of a residential training organised by the APPAS (Association pour la Promotion de l’Accompagnement Sexuel), a French sexual assistance organisation, near Strasbourg in March 2015. Sexual assistance for disabled people operates in France without legal recognition, between care work and commercial sex, a configuration that makes the mechanisms of occupational construction unusually visible. Drawing on participant observation, twenty-seven semi-structured interviews, forty-eight practitioner profiles and a press dossier, the article identifies three registers through which practitioners construct an occupational identity and a boundary: a legal register that converts the silence of the law into permissibility; a relational register that distinguishes accompaniment from commercial sex through reciprocity and time; and a subjective register that makes inner transformation the condition of competence. This structure is a form of proto-professionalisation characteristic of liminal care occupations, and it maps onto the three dimensions of occupational taint that sexual assistance accumulates at once. The registers do not resolve the taint: they convert it into a resource for jurisdictional claiming. The article extends Abbott’s account of jurisdictional work by showing that such claims can be made in the public arena, with the press as audience, before any settlement in the legal or workplace arenas, and by showing that occupational taint is converted rather than merely managed.
