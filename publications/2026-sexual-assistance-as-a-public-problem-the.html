Sexual assistance as a public problem : the politics of intimacy in contemporary France

Pierre Brasseur · French Politics, 2026

Abstract

How does an issue as intimate as sexuality reach the public agenda—and why, having reached it, does it sometimes produce no policy at all? Between 2007 and 2013, sexual assistance for people with disabilities achieved in France everything that agenda-setting theory identifies as success: three converged streams, sustained media attention, parliamentary reports, an opinion of the national ethics committee. No legislation followed. The multiple streams framework and its recent refinements explain such decoupling by the absence of a political entrepreneur able to assemble a legislative coalition. The French case makes that explanation insufficient: cause entrepreneurs were mobilized throughout, and mobilized effectively. Proceeding abductively from that surprise, the article develops the concept of legal path dependence—a cross-domain, discursive capture in which an adjacent legal category, the abolitionist proxénétisme framework, monopolizes the terms in which the new claim can be described, foreclosing decision coupling before political weighing begins. The analysis draws on archival research, over 2,500 documents, ethnographic observation of the first French sexual assistance training programme, and twenty stakeholder interviews.
